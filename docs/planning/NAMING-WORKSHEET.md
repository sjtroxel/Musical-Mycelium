## 1. Why the DIY attempt stalled

The complaint was "the name-space is crowded and juxtaposing Musical Maps every-which-way isn't getting there."
That's an accurate read of the situation, and the reason is mechanical, not a failure of imagination:

Every recombination was drawn from **the same two vocabularies everyone else in music tech draws from** — music words
(sound, tune, note, music, audio, sonic, beat) crossed with map words (map, atlas, chart, graph, guide, compass).
That intersection was exhausted a decade ago. The screen below confirms it: **every single plain dictionary word tested
is registered** — `stemma.com`, `descant.com`, `cognate.com`, `alluvial.com`, `antiphon.com`, `progenitor.com`,
`isogloss.com`, `recension.com`, `groundbass.com`. So are the obvious compounds: `soundlineage.com`,
`genregenealogy.com`, `headwatersmusic.com`.

The fix is not to try harder inside that intersection. It's to **change one of the two vocabularies.**

## 2. The generator

The project's actual subject is not "music" and not "maps." It is **descent from a common ancestor, reconstructed from
surviving evidence**. Several disciplines have spent centuries building precise vocabulary for exactly that idea, and
almost none of it has been claimed by music tech:

- **Manuscript studies / textual criticism** — reconstructing a family tree of copies from surviving witnesses. This is
  *the closest structural analog to the project that exists*, and nobody in music has taken it.
- **Historical linguistics** — how forms descend, diverge, and diffuse geographically. Same graph, different substance.
- **Phylogenetics** — descent with modification, drawn as a tree.
- **Music theory's own historical vocabulary** — not the pop-production words (beat, riff, drop), the *old* ones, which
  are about layering something new over something inherited.

That's the help worth giving: a generator, not a list. The lists below are what falls out of it.

## 3. His own naming grammar (worth knowing before choosing)

Nine prior project names: ChronoQuizzr, Poster Pilot, Asteroid Bonanza, Wildlife Sentinel, Heritage Odyssey,
Patchwork Assurance, SoilProve, Strawberry Star, Mighty Mileage Meetup.

The pattern is consistent and it is **not** obscure one-word Latin. It's **[concrete subject] + [role or vibe]**, two
words, approachable, frequently alliterative, sayable by a recruiter who has never seen it written down.

This matters because Family A below (Stemma, Cantus, Antecedent) is *conceptually* the strongest material here and
*stylistically* the furthest from anything he has ever named. Either is a defensible choice — but choosing a one-word
scholarly term is a deliberate break from his own grammar, and it should be made knowingly rather than by accident.

## 4. Candidates

### Family A — descent-from-evidence words (one-word, scholarly, high concept)

| Name | Why it fits | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Stemma** | A stemma is literally the family tree of a text reconstructed from surviving copies. Exact match to what the app does. Bonus: audio "stems" are multitrack layers, so the word reads musically too. Five letters, spellable, sayable. | **Real collision:** Stemma was a Seattle data-catalog startup (Sequoia-backed, 2020) acquired by Teradata in 2023. Brand is retired but it lived in *data tooling* — the one industry his audience works in. Near-collision, not fatal, but it's the honest flag. `stemma.com` gone. |
| **Descant** | A descant is a new line sung *over* an inherited melody — layering the new atop the old, which is the whole thesis of music history. Contains the ghost of "descent." Beautiful word. | Only small music-education businesses use it (a church-choir coaching co., a studio in Holyoke). No software collision found. `descant.com` gone; a compound works. |
| **Antecedent** | Double meaning: in music, the opening phrase that a consequent answers; in plain English, the thing that came before. | Four syllables. Slightly clinical. |
| **Cantus** | From *cantus firmus*, the fixed pre-existing melody everything else is built around — the through-line under the surface. | Reads Latin/liturgical; may skew the project churchward. `cantusfirmus.com` gone. |
| **Cognate** | Linguistics: forms descended from a shared ancestor. The exact claim the graph makes about genres. | `cognate.com` gone; a few small firms use it. `cognate.fm` is open. |
| **Continuo** | The basso continuo: the continuous bass under an entire era's music. | `continuo.fm` gone. Weakest availability of the family. |

### Family B — his own grammar, [subject] + [role]

All of these were screened; availability noted in §5.

- **Chorus Chronicle** — alliterative, instantly legible, says "history of music" in two words with no explanation.
- **Groove Genealogy** — most explicit about the family-tree idea; alliterative; slightly playful, in the Asteroid
  Bonanza register.
- **Cadence Codex** — a codex is a bound manuscript; pairs the music word with the manuscript-studies vocabulary.
  (Note: "Cadence" alone is heavily taken in tech — Cadence Design Systems, Uber's Cadence engine — but the two-word
  phrase doesn't collide.)
- **Motif Atlas** — a motif is the smallest inherited unit; atlas carries the spanning-all-eras claim.
- **Riff Lineage** — plainest and most modern-sounding of the set.
- **Sound Almanac** — almanac implies span and record-keeping rather than navigation.
- **Refrain Registry** — "registry" leans into the cited/sourced discipline, which is the actual differentiator.
- **Genre Loom** — a loom weaves separate threads into one fabric; the only metaphor here that captures *convergence*
  as well as descent, which matters because genres merge as often as they split.

### Family C — hybrids (Family A's concept in Family B's grammar)

These are the ones that get both the unclaimed vocabulary and his own sayable two-word shape:

- **Sound Stemma** / **Genre Stemma** / **Music Stemma**
- **Descant Graph**
- **Cantus Graph**
- **Continuo Graph**

### Family D — plain English, no metaphor

- **The Begat Project** — biblical genealogy cadence, blunt and memorable, and it makes people smile.
- Others in this register (mostly poor availability, listed for completeness): *Where It Came From*, *First Heard*,

## 6. The one upstream question

`09` §2 argues the product decision should precede the name, and it isn't settled: **A** question-answerer,
**B** explorable map, **C** guided tour. It changes which family reads right:

- If **A** (a search box, ask and get a cited lineage narrative) — registry/chronicle/almanac words fit; atlas and map
  words overpromise a visual interface that won't exist for months.
- If **B** (the graph *is* the interface) — atlas, cartography, loom, stemma all fit.
- If **C** (guided tour: "take me from delta blues to Detroit techno") — path and journey words fit, and none of the
  candidates above are aimed there yet.

This does not have to be a thirty-minute conversation to unblock naming. The version that matters for the name is one
question: **what does the first screen show before the user does anything — a search box, or a graph?**

## 7. How to pick

His own bar from `00` §6 and `09` §4:

1. Six-seconds-distinct from the other eight projects, and genuinely his.
2. Survives being said cold in an interview: "I built a thing called ___" without a spelling detour.
3. Doesn't near-collide with an active company, especially in music tech or data tooling.
4. It should be fun to type into a repo every day for three months.

Test that survives every naming session: say the top three out loud, in a full sentence, to nobody. The one that
doesn't make him wince is the name.
